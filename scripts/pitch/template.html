To see every young person have a real opportunity to follow Jesus by 2033.

How will we know?

Thirty-plus organisations. Dozens of countries. One bold goal — and, until now, no shared way to tell whether young people are actually following Jesus. Each of us counts our own outputs, alone. This is a proposal to change that: one shared measure, owned by everyone, that lets the whole movement finally see the same picture.

A draft proposal — for input and shaping

This draws on the extensive work of the Next Gen Global Collab, in consultation with the involved organisations and their seconded researchers — Ulrich Lombard, Eido Research, and Dr. Matthew Niermann. It is offered as a starting point, not a finished plan.Clay to be moulded — not a vase to be baked.

01Why the change?+

A clear-eyed look at the original 50-country study surfaced three walls that no amount of tweaking could move. As Matthew put it, we were adjusting the haircut budget when the rent was the problem.

  • The under-18 wall. Our research director cannot put his name on centrally-collected data for minors across many countries without enormous, multi-country ethics-board infrastructure. About 70% of Collab organisations work with under-18s — so an "18-and-over" headline doesn't serve the coalition.
  • The money wall. A robust 50-country longitudinal study cannot be done on our ~$1M budget. For scale: the Global Flourishing Study covers 22 countries and required ~$43M. One million dollars buys roughly one country for seven years — not fifty.
  • The quality wall. Forcing a global study onto a small budget means snowball sampling and ad-hoc collection — data we can't compare year to year or region to region. Serious research partners like OneHope were rightly hesitant to sign off on that.

The crowdsourced model removes all three at once: organisations gather their own data under their own consent models, so the cost, the legal load, and the Western-sampling bias all move off our central plate.

03A proven pattern — here are three+

For this to be a proven pattern, it has to be a pattern. It is. Across business, academia and the faith sector, the same play recurs: define one shared measure, validate it, give it away, and let everyone benchmark against it.

Business · workplace
Gallup Q12

Twelve questions, organised in a four-level hierarchy, used across 50+ industries — you run it and benchmark against a global database. Powers the annual State of the Global Workplace.

See Q12 →
Academia · global
Global Flourishing Study

Harvard's Human Flourishing Program + Baylor + Gallup. Its Secure Flourishing Index measures six domains across 200,000 people in 22 countries. The very study we cite as too costly to copy — but its shared index is exactly the model.

See the GFS →
Faith sector · children
World Vision · Hope & Love

A validated tool measuring how children experience God's love across six signs of hope — Compassion, Joy, Purpose, Resilience, Wisdom, Spirituality. Built with Harvard, Duke & UEL. Released 2025.

Announcement →   Full report →

And beyond these, the whole idea is everyday practice — the Net Promoter Score is a single number millions of organisations report and benchmark against. We learn the model, not the questions: where Q12 measures organisational growth, the Index measures gospel momentum across four tiers — have I encountered, responded, am I being shaped, does it spread?

04How the crowdsourced model works+

An organisation signs up and gets the Index white-labelled as their own — their logo, their survey, their results to keep. Their data flows back to the Index. When a country reaches a critical mass of crowdsourced responses, benchmarking switches on for that country.

  • The integrity line. We never claim to speak for a whole nation. We only ever say: "of those who have completed the Index, here are the results — and here's how you compare." That single discipline is what makes crowdsourced data responsible.
  • Tiered access. The measures are free and open to anyone. A standard report is free. An advanced report is paid — but free for Collab members. Coaching and consulting sit on top as a paid layer.
  • Risk moves to the edge. Because organisations run it themselves with their own consent models, central liability for under-18 data largely disappears.
05Who does what+

Still being finalised — presented here as the proposed shape, not a settled contract.

  • The Collab owns the project — and crucially, owns the relationships and the people. NXT Move continues as the appointed three-year backbone of the Collab.
  • Eido Research provides the engine — the survey infrastructure and the intelligence platform. This sits squarely in their wheelhouse; they're already building shared-measure platforms of exactly this kind, so we add one new standardised measure onto infrastructure others are already funding.
  • Co-owned IP — the Index would be jointly held, governed by a contract between NXT Move (for the Collab) and Eido Research. Anyone can publish results as long as both are credited.
  • Partner organisations participate — they run it, keep their own data, and get diagnostics back. The ask shifts from "give time to our research" to "use this and benefit."
06The numbers+

The budget doesn't change — the same ~$1M through 2033 that we've already taken to foundations. What changes is what it buys. The old plan would have spent the entire envelope on field research in a handful of countries and still come up short. The pivot fits comfortably inside $1M and funds the whole platform — the shared metric, the dashboard, distribution, and consulting — with room to scale as adoption grows.

These are realistic starting points, not a ceiling. Early years are lean because the crowdsourced model pushes data-gathering cost out to the partners; later years carry the dashboard build-out, translation, and the human layer.

  • 2027 · Prove the model. Activate a handful of key countries and NextGen Collab–engaged organisations to initiate real momentum and demonstrate that the crowdsourced model works at scale.

Suggested focus for next season

Generated from the gaps above — a starting point for the conversation, not a prescription.

1
Close the multiplication gap
Your young people believe and are being formed, but few are yet discipling others. Build simple, reproducible "each-one-reach-one" pathways and give every formed student one person to invest in.
2
Let Scripture challenge choices
The one area both below national and falling year-on-year. Worth a targeted focus on letting the Bible speak into everyday decisions, where culture is clearly pulling the other way.
3
Protect what's working
Prayer and "faith changes how I treat people" both jumped — name what you changed last year and keep doing it. Your strength is turning belief into a shaped life.
